Status: **raw brainstorm, not a commitment**. Purpose: cast a wide net
over "what could a todo/activity app do" and hold it up against what
dunnit/dunnit already *is* and *aims to be*, so gaps and non-fits are

## 0. Core identity (do not lose this while brainstorming)

Dunnit is NOT a task manager / project planner. It is a **low-friction,
factual activity ledger** with light forward-looking touches bolted
on. The center of gravity is always: "what did I actually do,"
recorded almost as fast as it happens, in plain text, without
friction, without hierarchy, without needing upkeep. Everything below
should be judged against: *does this reduce friction on capturing
reality, or does it start turning dunnit into a todo-list app with
projects/priorities/deadlines?* The latter is scope creep — dunnit
already deliberately isn't that (no Jira, no OKR tracker).

Three time-orientations already present, in priority order:
1. **Past** (primary): what did I do — the ledger, the `DONE`/`WIN`/
   `TIL`/`MEETING` entries, the recurring nudges.
2. **Near-future** (secondary): what will I do — `TODO` (small, tight,
   near-term items) and `GOAL` (bigger, overarching aims), see §5.
3. **Present/ambient** (tertiary, least developed so far): stray
   thoughts, prep needs, things surfacing *during* the day that aren't
   yet actions or completed work.

**Primary use cases, named explicitly:**
- **DSU (Daily Standup)** — dunnit should make "what did I do
  yesterday / what am I doing today" trivial to produce, ideally to
  the point of barely needing to think about it (see §9's mini-
  calendar auto-nudge idea).
- **Weekly and monthly status reports** — written primarily for
  yourself, but shaped so a version could be shared with a
  boss/colleagues with light editing.
- **Annual review** — a longer-horizon rollup (§11) built mostly from
  `IMPACT` and `MILESTONE` entries.
- **Meeting prep**, especially recurring 1x1s (§4).

**Terminology note:** all popups/prompts/reminders in dunnit are called
**nudges** — the hourly-ish capture nudge, the EOD nudge, the SOD
nudge, meeting-prep nudges, etc. Use this term consistently going
forward instead of "popup"/"prompt" where it reads naturally.

## 1. What dunnit already does (baseline, for reference)

- Periodic capture nudge → timestamped ledger line, tagged by category.
- Manual "log an entry" anytime (not just on the nudge).
- Raw ledger file browsing/editing (`$EDITOR` or OS opener).
- Config: working hours window, popup interval, lunch time.
- End-of-Day (EOD) nudge: summary, productivity rating, sentiment,
  tomorrow's goals.
- Summarize: pick day/month/quarter, gather ledger files, shell out to
  `gh copilot` for a natural-language summary, auto-copy to clipboard.
- Everything lives under one `DUNNIT_DIR`, expected to be a synced git
  repo.

## 4. Brainstorm: meeting/task prep

`MEETING` entries are **not** attendance logging. They're a scratch
space for an idea/note/agenda-item **for an upcoming meeting**, so
that by the time you sit for (say) a recurring 1x1, all the
`MEETING`-tagged notes accumulated since last time are already
collected as a ready-made agenda.

- [ ] **"Meeting prep" button** — prompts for a tag (e.g. `#jeff`,
      `#boss`) and logs a `MEETING` entry under that tag the moment an
      agenda-worthy thought occurs. No calendar needed.
- [ ] **Agenda view / pull command** — given a tag, show all
      accumulated `MEETING`-tagged lines since last viewed/cleared,
      formatted as a ready agenda. Needs a "since when" marker —
      candidate: last time this tag's agenda was pulled, stored
      per-tag (small piece of state, still simple).
- [x] "Walking into X, what do I need" checklist — this **is** what
      `MEETING` + the agenda view are for; no separate mechanism.
- [ ] **Post-meeting quick-capture** — still open whether this needs
      its own category or is just a tagged `DONE`/`TIL` entry.
- **Calendar integration: still declined for real calendars.** But
  see §9 below — a genuinely tiny, dunnit-native "ad hoc mini-calendar"
  (not a calendar *integration*) is now in scope, which changes the
  shape of this section materially.

## 5. Brainstorm: forward-looking (TODO vs GOAL) — resolved split

This round settled the `TODO`/`GOAL` question:

- **`TODO`** — a small, tight, near-term actionable item. Explicitly
  *encouraged* now (reversing the earlier "deferred, avoid it" stance)
  — real usage data showed `TODO` used heavily already (56 hits) and
  Micah now sees it as a good tight word for exactly the small-item
  case, as long as it doesn't grow project-management baggage
  (no due dates, no priority levels, no state machine beyond
  "logged" → "converted to DONE").
- **`GOAL`** — reserved for **bigger, overarching aims** (e.g.
  quarter-level or career-level intentions), not "what I'll do
  tomorrow." The EOD "seed tomorrow's items" feature and the Daybook
  list-with-convert-to-DONE feature (below) now target **`TODO`**
  primarily, since that's the tight daily-item case; `GOAL` is
  reviewed on a longer cadence instead (naturally fits SOM, §9).
- [ ] **Daybook shows open `TODO`s (and, less frequently, `GOAL`s)
      with one-click convert-to-`DONE`.** Converting a `TODO` (or a
      `GOAL`) writes a new `DONE` entry; whether the original
      `TODO`/`GOAL` line gets struck/marked or just left as historical
      record is a small remaining design choice, leaning toward
      "leave as-is, the new `DONE` line is the record of completion"
      to keep ledger lines append-only/immutable.
- [ ] **Carry-forward of unconverted `TODO`s** — still worth a simple
      way to re-list `TODO`s not converted by day's end into
      tomorrow's ledger (plain re-listing, no state machine).
- [ ] **`SOMEDAY`/"someday-maybe" bucket** — now a candidate *category*
      rather than a separate mechanism; see §12's new-categories list.

## 9. Start-of-Day (SOD) and Start-of-Month (SOM)

Naming convention, matching EOD: **SOD** (Start-of-Day), **SOM**
(Start-of-Month). "EOM review" and "Start-of-Month wizard" are **the
same concept** — unified here as SOM.

- [ ] **SOD nudge**, mirroring EOD's structure — confirmed dunnit had
      this (`Beginning-of-day` appears in real historical data).
      Likely contents: readback of today's `TODO`s (and any relevant
      `GOAL`s), chance to add/adjust before the day starts. Doubles as
      private standup prep.
- [ ] **SOM ("Start-of-Month wizard" = "EOM review", unified)** —
      a multi-step monthly sequence:
      - Review last month's digest/status report.
      - **Review and cleanup of `IDEA`/`SOMEDAY`-style entries** —
        Micah: SOM is where these get triaged (promote to `TODO`,
        promote to `GOAL`, or let go).
      - **Prompt explicitly for `IMPACT` and `MILESTONE` entries** —
        since these are annual-review fuel and easy to forget to log
        in the moment, SOM actively asks "anything impact/milestone
        worthy this month?" rather than relying on organic capture.
      - Set/revisit monthly-level `GOAL`s.

## 10. The two daily files — confirmed permanent, both hand-editable

Resolved this round: dunnit will have **two separate per-day files,
permanently, by design** — not a stopgap:
1. The **ledger** (`ledger-<YYYYMMDD>.txt`) — one-liners only, by
   deliberate constraint. This is the fast, structured, factual
   record.
2. A **daily summary doc** (markdown, e.g. `summary-<YYYYMMDD>.md`,
   naming TBD) — free-form prose, unstructured, for reflection/
   narrative.

**Both are always hand-editable, independently, at any time** — no
regeneration of one when the other changes, no derived/generated-only
file that gets clobbered. The workflow Micah wants (a middle ground
between dunnit's manual-only approach and dunnit's current LLM-only
approach): **the summary doc is LLM-drafted first (via the existing
`gh copilot` pipeline), then hand-edited/refined by the user** — the
LLM draft is a starting point, not the final artifact, and future
edits never get overwritten by re-generation. This effectively formalizes
what Summarize already does, but persists the result to a real file
instead of just clipboard, and makes clear it's meant to be edited
afterward, not treated as disposable output.

## 12. Category Usage Guide (updated — decisions + new candidates)

| Category | Intended use | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `DONE` | Something completed. The bread-and-butter capture answer. | Core, unchanged. |
| `TODO` | A small, tight, near-term actionable item. Now **encouraged** (reversing earlier "avoid it" stance). | **Decision made this round.** |
| `GOAL` | A bigger, overarching aim (quarter/career-level), reviewed on a longer cadence (naturally fits SOM). Not for "what I'll do tomorrow" anymore. | **Decision made this round** — narrowed scope vs earlier drafts. |
| ~~`BLOCKER`/`BLOCKED`~~ → `WAITING` | Something stopping progress or paused/waiting on a dependency. Heavily used historically (`BLOCKER`: 13 hits) despite being "deferred." | **Decided this round** — `BLOCKER` and `BLOCKED` are merged into the single `WAITING` category below rather than kept as a separate active/passive distinction; revived (no longer deferred). |
| `MEETING` | Scratch note/agenda-item for an *upcoming* meeting, ideally tag-scoped (e.g. `#boss`) so it can be pulled as a ready agenda. | Unchanged. |
| ~~`MTG`~~ | Duplicate of `MEETING`. | **Dropped this round** — use `MEETING` only. |
| `TIL` | "Today I Learned" — a small fact/insight. | Unchanged; doubles as stray-thought bucket with `IDEA`. |
| `WIN` | A notable positive outcome, bigger than routine `DONE`. | Unchanged; feeds status reports/annual review. |
| `IMPACT` | A specific, higher-weight accomplishment for performance/annual review framing. Sometimes scored (`IMPACT(3)`). | Unchanged; SOM will actively prompt for these (§9). |
| `MILESTONE` | A significant checkpoint, bigger than day-to-day `DONE`/`WIN`. Very rarely used (1 hit in sample). | Unchanged; SOM will actively prompt for these too, addressing the under-use. |
| `SUMMARY` | EOD free-text "how did today go" line. | Unchanged, auto-written by EOD. |
| `SENTIMENT` | EOD mood/sentiment rating. | Unchanged, auto-written by EOD. |
| `PRODUCTIVITY` | EOD 1-5 productivity score. | Unchanged, auto-written by EOD. |
| `IDEA` | A stray idea/thought that isn't yet a task or learning. | Kept; triaged at SOM (promote to `TODO`/`GOAL` or drop). |
| **`SOMEDAY`** *(new)* | An explicit "maybe later, not now" bucket — distinct from `IDEA` by being already framed as a *possible future action*, not just a passing thought. Reviewed/triaged at SOM alongside `IDEA`. | **New candidate**, addresses the "IDEA vs someday/maybe" ambiguity flagged last round by giving it its own category instead of overloading `IDEA`. |
| **`WAITING`** *(new, resolved)* | Something paused, waiting on an external dependency (a person, a reply, a deploy, etc.) — merges old `BLOCKER`/`BLOCKED` distinction into one settled category. | **Decided this round** — replaces `BLOCKER`, `BLOCKED`, and `HOLD` candidates; single settled name/category. |
| **`FIXME`** *(new, resolved)* | A known defect/issue noticed but not immediately fixed — engineering-flavored, akin to code `FIXME` comments. | **Decided this round** — settled on `FIXME` over `BROKEN`. |
| **`OPTIMIZE`** *(new, resolved)* | Flags something noticed as difficult/inefficient/worth improving later — distinct from `FIXME` (broken) vs "works but could be better." | **Decided this round** — settled on `OPTIMIZE` over `HARD`, kept as a full category rather than demoted to a `#tag`. |
| **`QUESTION`** *(new, resolved)* | Something you need to ask someone — distinct from a `MEETING` note (doesn't need to wait for a meeting) or a `TODO` (not something *you* do). | **Decided this round** — replaces earlier `ASK`/`QUESTION` pairing; settled on `QUESTION`. |
| **`KUDOS`** *(new, resolved)* | Logging when someone helped you or did something notable — raw material for later giving credit in status reports/reviews. | **Decided this round.** |
| **`RISK`** *(new, resolved)* | Something that could become a problem later, worth surfacing before it does — more forward-looking/preventive than `WAITING`. | **Decided this round.** |

Category list is now fully resolved — no more open naming decisions
remain in this table (category-list length itself is worth keeping an
eye on as a usability cost, but every individual name is settled).

## 14. Ad hoc mini-calendar for recurring meetings (new)

New idea this round, deliberately **not** a calendar integration (no
`.ics`, no `EventKit`, no external accounts) — a tiny, dunnit-native
recurring-event list:

- [ ] **Recurring meeting entry** — a minimal form: name/tag (e.g.
      `#dsu`, `#boss-1x1`), day-of-week, time-of-day. Supports exactly
      one recurrence shape (weekly, specific DOW+time) — no complex
      RRULE support, no exceptions/holidays handling, deliberately
      dumb/simple.
- [ ] **Pre-meeting nudge** — ~15 minutes before a configured
      recurring meeting's time, dunnit automatically surfaces the
      relevant content: for a `#dsu`-tagged recurring entry, that's
      the standup-formatted export of yesterday's `DONE`/`WIN` lines
      (§7) — "basically makes standups automatic... just read it
      instead of thinking through yesterday." For a `#boss-1x1`-tagged
      entry, that's the accumulated `MEETING`-tagged agenda for that
      tag (§4).
- [ ] This is a small, self-contained feature that meaningfully
      strengthens two other sections at once (§4's agenda view, §7's
      DSU export) without touching real calendars at all — worth
      treating as a fairly high-value, still-simple addition.

## 15. Explicitly out of scope

- Project/epic hierarchies, priorities, due-date scheduling, Gantt/
  kanban views.
- Multi-user / team features.

  the mini-calendar in §14 is intentionally not this.

  pull` before you start"; true multi-device sync is a possible
  *future* SQLite-based redesign, not near-term.
